# Ten minutes

The compressed version, for a conference room. `WORKSHOP.md` is the real thing —
90 minutes, six questions on paper, the constraints and why they exist. Read it
this week. Right now you have ten minutes, so skip all of it and do this.

The goal for the next ten minutes is **not** a good tool. It is one change you
made, working, in front of you. That is the only thing that makes any of this
feel possible afterwards.

---

## 1 · Open the file (1 min)

Double-click **`maths-tool-starter.html`**. It's a number line with jumps on it.
Change the step to 0.25. Change it to −3. That's a working tool, one file, no
install, nothing to sign up for.

If you only downloaded that one file and not the whole folder, you have
everything you need. It carries its own instructions.

## 2 · Decide the one thing (2 min)

Not a maths website. **One lesson, one class, one thing that is hard.**

> "Year 8 period 5 cannot see why −7 − (−3) isn't −10."

Then write the test — the sentence that tells you it worked:

> "I can set it to −7 − (−3) and the jump on the number line goes *right*, not left."

If you can't write that sentence, pick something smaller. You have eight minutes.

## 3 · Hand it over (1 min)

Drag `maths-tool-starter.html` into a new Claude chat. Paste this, with your
own words in the brackets:

> **[Context]** This is my starter file. Read the brief in the comment at the top
> and follow those rules.
>
> **[The change]** Change it from number line jumps to **[YOUR THING]**.
>
> **[The test]** It works when I can **[YOUR SENTENCE FROM STEP 2]**.
>
> **[The limits]** Keep it one self-contained file, no libraries, keep the
> `:root` design tokens, and don't add anything I didn't ask for. Give me back
> the whole file.

Four parts: context, the change, the test, the limits. Drop one and you'll spend
the rest of the session correcting.

## 4 · Save it, open it, check it (6 min)

Save what comes back over a **copy** of the file — not the original. Open it in
the browser.

Then the part everyone skips: **check the maths by hand.** Not "does it look
right". Work out three of them yourself. AI-generated maths tools are plausible
first and correct second.

When something is wrong, report it like you'd report it to a colleague — lead
with the failing case:

> "Question 7 came out as 8 ÷ 3, which isn't a whole number. All the divisions
> should come out exactly. Fix just that."

Not "fix the divisions". The specific broken example is the most useful thing
you own.

---

## If you get stuck

Say so, plainly, in the chat. "I don't know what any of this means, walk me
through it like I've never seen a computer" gets you further than pretending.
Being your dumbest self is a technique, not a confession.

## If you hit a usage limit

You're on the free plan and it ran out. That's normal and it isn't you. Look at
the screen of the person next to you for the rest of the session, and pick it up
at home.
